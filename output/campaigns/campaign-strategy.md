### How filters work here (honest adaptation)

Rave sources from Google Maps through Localpipe, not LinkedIn or Clay. Everything in the "List filters" column is one of:

- **Localpipe `/maps-search` filters (free to scrape, about $1.00 per 1,000):** `has_website`, `min_rating`, `min_reviews`, `max_reviews`, category, state or city. Returned fields: name, phone, address, city, rating, review_count, website, types, price_level, place_id, is_claimed, working_hours.
- **Localpipe `/scrape-reviews-api` (free):** reviewer name, review text and "X months ago" for up to 40 newest reviews per place_id.
- **Localpipe `/enrich` (paid, $0.005 business email, $0.02 owner email):** owner name, owner email, email provider.
- **Free homepage fetch (Python or curl):** HTTPS, copyright year, generator and script fingerprints (wixstatic, godaddy, weebly), nav links, a missing gallery page.
- **Free public sources:** Google PageSpeed Insights (free key), NOAA Storm Events CSVs and FEMA OpenFEMA (no key), Google Ads Transparency Center (manual, scraping unofficial), RDAP, state business registries, public association member pages.

Anything needing LinkedIn, Sales Navigator, Clay or paid BuiltWith bulk is not used. Rows marked "Unverified" need a 20-minute check before a build.

**Copy rule for every idea:** value props and personalization use only `context/messaging/facts.md` (variant A/B lines, "up by next week", "I'll set it up and pay for it myself", "I'm not an agency, it's just me", the trades line, the three-things design principle). Facts about the prospect (their review, their footer year, their site's HTTPS) are observed live and are fine. Market stats from `signals-and-market.md` (BrightLocal 54% and so on) are NOT in facts.md, so they stay out of copy until Neil adds them. Never claim a per-company mockup already exists before they reply.

- Why it works without AI: the offer is the proof (a free finished design for their trade), tight targeting by trade and rating does the relevance work, and the email stays short and plain.

## Front-End Offer Suggestions

1. **Free finished sample design for the trade (already the offer).** The owner judges it in seconds. Rave never claims a company-specific mockup exists before they reply, so the free thing offered is a trade-specific sample plus a quote for their company.
2. **"I'll pay for the setup" (concept B).** Removes the $2,000 shock by moving the up-front risk to Rave. Test first as a follow-up line, not the opener.
3. **One-page "what's costing you jobs" note** (3 plain observations from their live site, the engine in #4). A softer first step than a design, useful when they are skeptical of "scam". It must never grade the owner, only the site.
4. **Before and after Google numbers (later).** Not available. Becomes possible after 3 proof sites (case-studies.md plan: permission, testimonial, 30-day numbers). Do not promise it now.

## Decisions made without review

Non-interactive run, so these choices were made here and are open for Neil to overrule:

1. **Counted the 4 existing concepts as ideas 1, 2, 5, 9,** credited as existing, in broad-to-niche order. Concept B was put at Tier 2 because it is a risk-reversal test on top of another list, not its own audience.
2. **Mother Intent for events with no catalog entry:** storms (#12) were tagged "Regulatory changes" as the closest catalog fit and flagged as a new intent "Weather or disaster event". Review velocity (#21) was tagged "Companies in hypergrowth (headcount)" with a proxy note. Most trade ideas fall under "Niche-specific play" because the catalog is B2B-software shaped.
3. **New Hire idea (#17)** was adapted from "person started a role" to "business newly opened or changed owner", because owner-run trades have no hiring-date data and LinkedIn is not used. Volume will be low.
4. **Lookalike seed (#18)** is the single water damage replier's profile, not a customer, because Rave owns no case study. This is thin; it needs the 3 proof sites to become a real lookalike.
5. **Creative non-ICP** has two ideas (referral partners and professional practices) because the skill asks for an adjacent audience and neither has strong evidence. Both are Tier 3 and need Neil's decisions on partner terms and a new sample design.
6. **Tiering:** Tier 1 went to ideas that use pools Rave already holds (state.md) or need no new data, plus the free site-gap ranking. Ideas that depend on repeat pulls, messy matching, unofficial scrapes or unverified Localpipe behavior are Tier 3.
7. **Market stats were kept out of value props** (BrightLocal 54% visit a website after positive reviews, and others), because facts.md does not authorize them. If Neil wants the 54% figure in copy, it must be added to facts.md with its source.
8. **No external research or paid calls were run.** All site, storm, ads and registry details come from `signals-and-market.md` and `localpipe-api-notes.md`, with their Confirmed, Likely and Unverified labels carried over where stated.
9. **Not read because absent at the time:** `context/docs/research/customer-voice.md`, `context/icp/signal-matrix.md`, `context/positioning/`. Re-run or revise once customer-voice.md lands; it may change which review and pain hooks are strongest.
